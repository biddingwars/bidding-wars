# US States: fun facts and where each one comes from

Each state has one kid-friendly fun fact, shown on the SOLD screen and in the results. A fact never mentions
population or population rank (population stays hidden until the end), and none is about politics.

## Verification status

**All 50 facts were checked online in October 2026** against the sources listed (NPS, state agencies, Census, USGS, NASA, the Coca-Cola Company, Mount Washington Observatory and similar). The first 37 were checked in the first pass; the last 13 (Florida, Indiana, Iowa, Louisiana, Nebraska, New Mexico, New York, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wisconsin) in a second pass, and all 13 held. One detail was not returned by the search: New Mexico's "Big Room" (a standard feature of Carlsbad Caverns; the bat flight at dusk was confirmed).
Four facts were reworded in the first pass because the check showed a claim the sources do not support as written:
- **Missouri**: "tallest arch in the world" is only on third-party sites; the National Park Service says "tallest man-made monument in the United States".
- **New Jersey**: the 1846 Hoboken game is the first *recorded match between two clubs*; "first organized game" is contested.
- **North Carolina**: the flights were made at Kill Devil Hills, about four miles from Kitty Hawk, so the fact now says "near Kitty Hawk".
- **Rhode Island**: "about 1,200 square miles" mixes sources (total area is about 1,545, land about 1,034 square miles); it now says land area.
Other findings kept as written: Mississippi (sources agree on Ojibwe *misi-ziibi*; the usual gloss is "great river", one source says "long river"), Idaho (212 ft vs Niagara's Horseshoe Falls 167 ft; Niagara's drop is quoted as 158-188 ft depending on flow), Grand Canyon (NPS says 277 river miles in older pages, 278 in the 2024 profile), Connecticut River (410 miles, Britannica says 407), Great Salt Lake (several times saltier than the ocean, depends on the arm and lake level), Tennessee ("Music City": the nickname is real, who coined it is disputed), Indiana (the Indy 500 is on Memorial Day weekend, the last weekend of May).

Languages: English only. The other four languages show the English fact (the engine falls back automatically).
Translating them is straightforward once the English wording is reviewed.
